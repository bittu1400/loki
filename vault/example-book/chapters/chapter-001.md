# Chapter 1 — Nine Pieces

The tide brought the glass in at dusk, as it always did above the cut-water, and Ovist Rhoam was on the office steps to meet it because no one else would be.

Driftglass formed only where the tide crossed old stone, and Vhal Mirek was nothing but old stone. The pieces came up the slip like coins spilled from a drowned purse, and each one held an echo of somebody who had gone under. Most echoes were ordinary. A woman naming her boat twice. A boy counting to forty in a language the city had forgotten. Ovist logged them in the Almanac by lamplight, in the small neat hand the Office paid him for, and did not think about whose voice he was filing.

Tonight there were nine pieces. He carried them inside past the Rope Walk door and set them on the ledger shelf, and it was there, sorting them by weight, that he found the page already turned.

The spring-tide entry carried two corrections. Both were in his hand. He had made neither.

He stood a long time with the page open while the tide went out without him, and somewhere below the slip a diver's lamp came on, small and yellow, sinking.
