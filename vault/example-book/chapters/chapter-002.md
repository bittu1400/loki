# Chapter 2 — A Falling Glass

Brannec Tull took the ebb down with a weighted line and a grudge, because the glass was falling and everyone knew you did not dive a falling glass, and everyone except Brannec had the sense to stay up.

Nine fathoms, then the ledge. The drowned quarter spread out below him like a street plan for a city that had lost the argument with the river. He worked fast. Unlogged driftglass fetched double from certain buyers, and Sela Vosk paid triple if nobody at the Office ever saw it first, which suited Brannec, who had been unseen at the Office for eleven years.

He pried four pieces loose and put them in the mesh bag, and on the fifth his glove caught on iron — old iron, square-nailed, wrong for this quarter — and when he looked closer the iron was a staple holding a lead seal to the stone. A seal from the Office. Dated this spring.

It could not be dated this spring. This quarter flooded once a generation.

He surfaced with the seal in his fist and did not tell the lamp-keeper, and did not tell Sela Vosk either, not yet. First he meant to find out who had been down here before him with an Office chisel and a reason.
